StreamTech Announces Restructuring, Lays Off 30% of Workforce

Seattle, WA - December 18, 2025

StreamTech, a video streaming platform startup, announced today it is laying off approximately 180 employees, representing 30% of its 600-person workforce, as part of a major restructuring effort.

The layoffs primarily affect the company's engineering, product, and sales teams. Affected employees will receive severance packages including 12 weeks of pay plus one week for each year of service.

"This was an incredibly difficult decision," said Tom Richardson, CEO of StreamTech. "We've made the tough choice to right-size our operations to focus on our core business and path to profitability."

The company, which raised $120M in Series C funding in 2023, has struggled to gain market share against larger competitors like Netflix and YouTube. Revenue growth has slowed significantly in 2025, leading to increased pressure from investors to reduce costs.

As part of the restructuring, StreamTech will also be consolidating its office footprint. The company currently occupies 85,000 square feet across two floors in downtown Seattle but plans to downsize to a single floor of approximately 40,000 square feet.

"By reducing our headcount and consolidating our office space, we expect to cut our annual operating expenses by approximately $35M," said CFO Linda Park. "These changes position us for sustainable growth moving forward."

The company's San Francisco satellite office, which employed 45 people, will be completely shut down by February 2026, with remaining employees given the option to relocate to Seattle or accept severance packages.

StreamTech also announced it is pausing all new hiring indefinitely and canceling plans for a previously announced Austin, Texas office that was scheduled to open in Q1 2026.

This marks the second round of layoffs for StreamTech this year. The company previously cut 50 positions in June 2025.

Several StreamTech competitors have also announced layoffs in recent months as the streaming market faces increased saturation and economic headwinds.

The company stated it will focus on its core subscription streaming service and plans to shut down several experimental features and products that were not gaining traction.

"While these changes are painful, they give us a clearer path forward," Richardson added. "We remain confident in our long-term vision for StreamTech."

Affected employees were notified this morning and will have access to career transition support services.
