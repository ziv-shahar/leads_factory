10 Lessons I Learned in 2025 as a Software Engineer

By Sarah Martinez | December 18, 2025

Personal reflections on career growth

As 2025 comes to a close, I wanted to share some personal lessons I've learned this year working as a senior software engineer. These are just my thoughts and experiences - your mileage may vary!

1. Code Review is About Learning, Not Criticism

I used to dread code reviews, but I've learned to embrace them as learning opportunities. Getting feedback on my pull requests has made me a better developer.

2. Take More Breaks

I discovered that taking regular breaks actually improves my productivity. The Pomodoro Technique changed my workflow completely.

3. Invest in Your Tools

I finally upgraded to a mechanical keyboard and a second monitor. The productivity boost was immediate. Don't cheap out on the tools you use every day!

4. Documentation Matters

Writing good documentation isn't glamorous, but it saves so much time in the long run. Future you will thank present you for good docs.

5. Learn in Public

Starting this blog was scary, but sharing my learning journey has connected me with amazing developers around the world. Don't be afraid to show your work!

6. Work-Life Balance is Real

I learned to set boundaries and actually take my vacation days. Burnout is real, and your employer will survive if you take time off.

7. Side Projects Should Be Fun

I stopped forcing myself to work on "impressive" side projects and started building things I actually enjoy. It's been so much more rewarding.

8. Mentoring is Rewarding

I started mentoring junior developers this year. Teaching others has reinforced my own understanding and given me a new perspective.

9. Imposter Syndrome Never Really Goes Away

Even as a senior engineer, I still sometimes feel like I don't know what I'm doing. I've learned that everyone feels this way sometimes, and it's okay.

10. Gratitude Goes a Long Way

Thanking my teammates, celebrating small wins, and acknowledging good work has made our team culture so much better.

Looking Forward to 2026

Next year, I want to dive deeper into distributed systems, contribute more to open source, and maybe speak at a conference. We'll see how it goes!
